Scope of Work

What the Conversion
Involves

The conversion is a major "firewall-forward" and cockpit modernization. Understanding the full scope helps your shop plan resources and scheduling.

  • Remove Original G 2500 engine, instrument panel, and associated engine systems
  • Laminate Bulkhead electrical breakthroughs — specialized laminating work on the front firewall structure
  • Install New Rotax 912iSc3 powerplant, engine mount, and MT propeller with unfeathering accumulator
  • Install Digital avionics suite, new fuel lines, coolant system, and LED landing-light cowling
  • Verify Electrical and fuel system checkouts, formal engine run-up, and new aircraft weighing
  • Record All results in the updated Aircraft Flight Manual and maintenance documentation
Consumables not included in the kit. Your shop is responsible for standard consumables such as adhesives, fabrics, lubricants, coolants, sealants, and paints, as well as the necessary tools and jigs required for the installation.

Technical FAQ for Maintenance Shops

Common Questions
from Installation Partners

What is the full scope of work for the maintenance shop? +
The conversion is a major "firewall-forward" and cockpit modernization. It includes removing the original G 2500 engine and instrument panel, performing specific laminating work for bulkhead electrical breakthroughs, and installing the new Rotax systems, digital avionics, and fuel lines. The aircraft must then be weighed and the Aircraft Flight Manual updated before return to service.
What is excluded from the kit that our shop must supply? +
The shop is responsible for standard consumables such as adhesives, fabrics, lubricants, coolants, sealants, and paints. You will also need to provide the necessary tools and jigs required for the installation. All major mechanical and avionics components are included in the Option A kit supplied by Grob Aircraft SE.
How is the installation verified before returning the aircraft to service? +
After mechanical installation, the shop must conduct a series of preliminary electrical and fuel system checkouts, followed by a formal engine run-up and a new aircraft weighing procedure. All results — including the new empty weight and center of gravity — must be recorded in the updated Aircraft Flight Manual before the aircraft can return to service.
