As a global leader in flight training systems, Grob Aircraft SE is dedicated to redefining the boundaries of the cockpit. Our strategic trajectory encompasses the cutting edge of military aviation and autonomous technology, exemplified by our partnership with Helsing to unveil the CA-1 Europa — an autonomous fighter jet designed to operate in the most contested environments. This evolution is driven by a singular purpose: the precision and engineering rigor required for our next-generation autonomous combat systems informs our approach to legacy fleet sustainability, ensuring that every airframe we support meets the technological standards of the 21st century.

"To shape the future of the cockpit by integrating advanced autonomous systems with proven aerospace engineering, ensuring our training systems remain the global benchmark for excellence."

Case Study in Legacy Preservation: The G 109B Modernization

The G 109B motor glider has been a cornerstone of the Grob family for nearly 40 years. Its robust airframe is capable of many more decades of service; however, the original G 2500 engine is now facing critical parts obsolescence. To ensure this popular touring motor glider remains future-proof, Grob has engineered an EASA-certified modernization kit that replaces the aging powerplant with the advanced Rotax 912iSc3 system.

Technical Deep Dive: The Rotax 912iSc3 Conversion Kit

The conversion is supplied as a comprehensive kit (P/N K/OSB817-071) that addresses every critical system affected by the engine upgrade.

Powerplant & Control

  • Engine — Rotax 912iSc3 Sport featuring 4 cylinders, liquid cooling, and electronic fuel injection.
  • Propeller System — MT-Propeller MTV-21-A-C-F/CF170-O5 (1.7 m diameter) equipped with a hydraulic governor and a pressure accumulator for normal positioning.
  • Fuel System — 'Twin-Pack' electric fuel pump providing double redundancy, including new fuel filters and supply/return hoses.
  • Controls — New high-precision Bowden cables for throttle, propeller position, and cabin heating.

Avionics & Electrical

  • Display — Kanardia Digi-F digital engine display integrated with a DAQU (Data Acquisition) unit for real-time monitoring.
  • Panel — A redesigned right instrument panel equipped with modern switches and a master key switch.
  • Electrical — 912iSc3-specific fuse box, new starter battery with higher capacity and a dedicated mount, and an external power supply plug.

Airframe & Cooling

  • Cowling — Newly designed engine cowling featuring an integrated high-power LED landing light.
  • Mounts — Specialized engine mount, firewall, and vibration dampers.
  • Cooling — Optimized air ducts and a water cooling system including a thermostat, radiator, and heating box.

Performance Gains: More Power, More Comfort, More Fun

The transition to the Rotax 912iSc3 provides measurable improvements in aircraft performance and operational flexibility.

  • Power Increase — A +12.5% increase in overall power and a 5.3% increase specifically for take-off.
  • Speed Efficiency — A 5% increase in cruise speed at 5,000 ft.
  • Short Field Operations — Take-off distance is reduced by 20% at sea level (ISA), requiring only 250 m.
  • Fuel Versatility — The engine is certified for Avgas 100 LL, EN 228 Super, Super-Plus, and environmentally friendly E10.
Pilot's Advantage

The integration of the ECU represents a paradigm shift in workload reduction for the recreational pilot. The system automatically optimizes fuel consumption and engine parameters, ensuring a 250 m take-off distance and easier handling with fewer manual adjustments required during flight.

Ensuring Airworthiness: Rigorous Testing and Documentation

Safety and regulatory compliance are the foundations of Service Bulletin OSB 817-71. The technical content is approved under the authority of DOA ref. EASA.21J.030 and relates to Change no. OÄM817-030, approved by EASA under Approval no. 10076612.

  1. Weight and CG Determination — Upon installation, a new aircraft weight and center of gravity must be determined and recorded in the Aircraft Flight Manual (AFM).
  2. System Verification — Mandatory dynamic fuel leak checks must be performed according to AMM 28-20 procedures with a minimum of 30 L of fuel.
  3. Engine Run-Up & FADEC Verification — A ground test protocol is required to confirm system integrity:
    • ECU Channel Check: verification that RPM change does not exceed 250 when switching between ECU channels at 4,000 RPM.
    • Electrical Load Test: verification that voltage indicates approximately 14 VDC once the external alternator engages.
    • Propeller Bleeding: three cycles to bleed air from the hydraulic system and verify governor stabilization.
