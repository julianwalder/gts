Training Architecture

Modern pilot training requires more than a standalone simulator

Many training organizations continue to rely on simulation infrastructures originally designed to support procedural training as separate devices. Today, training requirements increasingly include:

  • aircraft-specific cockpit familiarization
  • earlier reduction of training risk
  • mission planning and debriefing
  • realistic operational environments
  • continuity between simulation and aircraft operations

The Grob training ecosystem addresses these requirements with a type-specific architecture built around the Grob G120TP.

Legacy Simulation vs. Integrated Training Ecosystem
Legacy Simulation
  • isolated training device
  • procedural focus only
  • limited mission context
  • weak connection to aircraft operations

Type-Specific Design

A high-fidelity twin of the Grob G120TP

All Grob training devices are based on the Grob G120TP. They are not generic simulators adapted to multiple aircraft types. Across the ecosystem, pilots train with the same cockpit logic, avionics structure and procedural architecture as the aircraft platform.

This type-specific approach supports:

  • earlier cockpit familiarity
  • stronger procedural consistency
  • more efficient transfer from simulation to flight
  • a closer connection between training and operations

Operational Environment
