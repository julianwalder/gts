Breathing New Life into a Legend:
The Grob G 109B's High-Tech Evolution

Grob Aircraft SE  ·  Technical Editorial

The Pilot's Dilemma

The Grob G 109B has long been celebrated as a robust touring motor glider, featuring an airframe designed with a sophisticated industrial glass fiber and carbon fiber architecture. However, four decades after its debut, many owners are finding that while their airframes remain structurally pristine, the legacy Grob G 2500 engines have reached their 1,600-hour Time Between Overhaul (TBO) several times over. This creates a critical crossroads: retire a beloved icon or find a way to modernize a powertrain that is increasingly starved for spare parts.

The Rotax 912iSc3 Sport conversion isn't just a replacement; it is a heart transplant for a legendary airframe. By marrying a flight-proven glider design with contemporary propulsion technology, Grob Aircraft SE has created a pathway for this aircraft to excel in a new era. This modernization ensures that the G 109B remains a top-tier performer, offering a seamless blend of classic handling and modern reliability.

From Obsolete to Cutting-Edge: Why Change Was Mandatory

The release of Service Bulletin OSB817-71 marks a pivotal moment for the global G 109B fleet, shifting the narrative from maintenance to modernization. The primary driver for this change is the "obsolescence of parts" for the original G 2500 engine, which has made continued operation of the legacy unit an exercise in diminishing returns. For owners, this bulletin turns the threat of permanent grounding into an opportunity to future-proof their investment.

"Due to the obsolescence of parts for the original G 2500 engine, the Rotax 912iSc3 has been certified as an alternative engine."

This certification ensures that the aircraft remains fully compliant and supportable under the authority of the DOA ref. EASA.21J.030. By moving away from an unsupported engine platform, owners can effectively reset the clock on their aircraft's operational utility. This shift transforms a legacy machine into a high-performance modern motorglider that meets current certification standards.

Performance by the Numbers: Shorter, Faster, Stronger

The technical centerpiece of this evolution is the 100 SHP Rotax 912iSc3 Sport engine, paired with the advanced MTV-21-A-C-F/CF170-05 hydraulically controlled constant speed propeller. This combination delivers a 12.5% increase in overall power and a 5.3% boost specifically for take-off performance. These gains are further amplified by a lower total weight, a critical advantage that improves the aircraft's useful load and climb gradients.

The most striking improvement is the 20% reduction in take-off distance, which drops to a mere 250 meters under ISA conditions at Sea Level. This allows pilots to operate safely from shorter strips or navigate high-density altitude environments with a renewed margin of safety. Additionally, the G 109B now enjoys a 5% increase in cruise speed at 5,000 feet, making cross-country touring more efficient than ever before.

The Fuel Flexibility Revolution

In an era where fuel availability and environmental impact are at the forefront of aviation discussions, the new engine offers unprecedented flexibility. The Rotax 912iSc3 Sport can operate without restriction on Avgas 100 LL, EN 228 Super, Super-Plus, and even the environmentally friendly E10. This variety allows operators to source fuel from standard automotive stations when traditional aviation fuel is unavailable or prohibitively expensive.

The ability to use E10 is a genuine game-changer for modern cost-efficiency and sustainability in the cockpit. It significantly lowers the hourly operating costs while reducing the lead emissions associated with traditional Avgas. This flexibility, combined with the precision of a modern injection system, ensures the G 109B is ready for a changing regulatory landscape.

Silicon Meets the Sky: The ECU Advantage

The transition to the Rotax 912iSc3 Sport moves the G 109B from legacy manual engine management into the digital age. The integrated Electronic Control Unit (ECU) optimizes fuel consumption in real-time and handles the complex adjustments that once required constant pilot attention. This reduction in workload allows the pilot to focus on the joy of flight rather than managing mixture and temperature parameters.

Inside the cockpit, the modernization is completed by the Kanardia Digi-F digital engine display and its accompanying DAQU unit. This interface replaces cluttered analog gauges with a clean, intuitive display of all vital engine health data. The result is a simplified, modern flight deck experience that enhances situational awareness and provides a level of technical authority previously unseen in this class.

More Than Just a Motor: The Complete Transformation Kit

Grob delivers this modernization as a comprehensive "re-kit" (P/N K/OSB817-071) designed to refresh the entire aircraft system. Rather than a simple engine swap, the kit includes redesigned systems that improve safety, electrical capacity, and even the aircraft's aesthetic profile. Every component has been meticulously engineered to integrate with the existing fiber-reinforced airframe.

Key components of the transformation kit include:

  • 'Twin-Pack' Fuel System — Dual electric fuel pumps providing two-fold redundancy for maximum operational safety.
  • Upgraded Electrical Suite — A new starter battery with higher capacity and a dedicated 912iSc3 fuse box for digital reliability.
  • Redesigned Cowling — A new, white-painted engine cover that includes an integrated high-power LED landing light for better visibility.
  • Advanced Propeller System — The MTV-21 constant speed propeller equipped with a hydraulic governor and accumulator for seamless pitch control.

The Next 40 Years of Flight

The Grob G 109B airframe was always ahead of its time, built with a philosophy of endurance that has outlasted its original powerplant. By addressing the obsolescence of the G 2500, this high-tech evolution ensures that the rest of the aircraft finally matches the standard of its industrial glass fiber and carbon fiber design. Owners can now look forward to many more years and thousands of hours of worry-free, high-performance flying.

As we look toward the future of general aviation, the G 109B serves as a masterclass in fleet modernization. It raises a compelling question for every owner of a classic aircraft: is it better to buy a new, unproven design, or to invest in the preservation of a legendary airframe through the application of world-class modern technology? For those who value the heritage of the Grob G 109B, the choice to modernize is the most sustainable path to the sky.
