Technical Evaluation Report:
G 109B Engine Modernization
G 2500 to Rotax 912iSc3

Grob Aircraft SE  ·  Engineering Report  ·  Service Bulletin OSB817-71

1. Project Background and Strategic Necessity

The Grob G 109B has served as a cornerstone of the global training and touring motorglider fleet for nearly four decades. While the robust composite airframe remains viable for extended service, the legacy propulsion systems have reached a critical juncture. The original Grob G 2500 engine, which carries a 1,600-hour Time Between Overhaul (TBO) that many units have surpassed multiple times, now suffers from terminal part obsolescence.

To ensure the fleet's continued airworthiness, Grob Aircraft SE has issued Service Bulletin OSB817-71, establishing an EASA-approved alternative engine installation. This shift replaces aging, high-maintenance hardware with a modern propulsion standard designed to secure the airframe's utility for the next 40 years.

Statement of Purpose

The primary catalyst for this modernization is the acute part obsolescence of the G 2500, a factor that threatens the long-term viability of the G 109B. The overarching goal is a "future-proof modernization" through Change No. OÄM817-030. By transitioning to the Rotax 912iSc3, operators move from a legacy maintenance burden to a high-reliability, OEM-certified configuration that significantly improves handling, safety, and performance.

This strategic upgrade is not merely an engine swap but a comprehensive system transformation, leveraging the technical superiorities of the Rotax 912iSc3 powerplant to redefine the aircraft's operational profile.

2. Comparative Powerplant Analysis: G 2500 vs. Rotax 912iSc3

In aeronautical engineering, the powerplant is the core driver of aircraft utility. Moving from the G 2500 to the Rotax 912iSc3 represents a technological leap from 1980s-era mechanical systems to a 21st-century electronically controlled, fuel-injected architecture. This shift transforms the G 109B into a modern asset by replacing manual pilot-adjusted systems with automated electronic optimization.

Impact of Hydraulic Constant Speed Control

The transition from 87 PS to 100 SHP provides a 12.5% increase in total rated power, offering substantially higher safety margins during high-density altitude operations. Furthermore, the upgrade to the MTV-21 hydraulic constant speed system — supported by a dedicated pressure accumulator for normal positioning — eliminates the manual complexity of the legacy Hoffmann prop, ensuring the engine always operates at the optimal RPM for the given flight phase.

3. Operational Efficiency and Environmental Flexibility

In current aviation markets, fuel versatility and consumption management are vital for controlling cost-per-hour and mitigating environmental impact. The Rotax 912iSc3 introduces a level of flexibility that was previously unattainable with legacy mechanical systems.

While the legacy G 2500 was restricted to AVGAS 100 LL or high-octane automobile fuel (Min ROZ 96.0), the Rotax engine is compatible with:

  • AVGAS 100 LL
  • EN 228 Super and Super-Plus
  • Environmentally friendly E10

Central to this efficiency is the Electronic Control Unit (ECU). The ECU automates fuel consumption across all flight phases, effectively eliminating the need for manual mixture leaning. Crucially, the ECU integration removes legacy failure points such as the Manual Choke and Carburetor Heat requirements — formerly items 9 and 11 on the legacy AFM cockpit checklist — simplifying the pilot's workload and reducing the risk of engine icing or improper mixture settings.

Compatibility with E10 and automotive fuels is a significant logistical advantage in regions where AVGAS is being phased out or is prohibitively expensive. This fuel flexibility enhances the aircraft's standing in an increasingly regulated environmental landscape, making it a more sustainable and cost-effective choice for fleet operators and flight schools.

Expansion of the Operational Envelope

A 20% reduction in take-off distance is transformative for a motor glider. It expands the G 109B's operational envelope to shorter runways and grass strips that were previously marginal, increasing its versatility as a touring platform. Additionally, the 5% increase in cruise speed at 5,000 ft translates to improved cross-country touring efficiency, allowing for longer legs and reduced time-in-transit.

5. Technical Integration and System Redundancy

Modern flight operations demand high levels of system safety and redundancy. The transition from legacy single-ignition systems to the dual-channel electronic Rotax 912iSc3 represents a professional standard in reliability.

Critical Components — Kit P/N K/OSB817-071

  • ECU Redundancy — The engine features two independent channels, Lane A and Lane B. This shifts the pre-flight routine from a traditional magneto test to a modern ECU Lane check, ensuring dual-channel integrity before departure.
  • Fuel System Safety — The kit includes a "Twin-Pack" electrical fuel pump assembly with 2-fold redundancy, ensuring continuous fuel delivery.
  • Advanced Monitoring — The integration of the Kanardia Digi-F digital engine indication system, paired with a DAQU (Data Acquisition Unit), provides the pilot with precise, real-time telemetry.
  • Electrical Overhaul — The modernization includes a new 912iSc3 fuse box and a high-capacity starter battery, housed within a redesigned cowling featuring high-power LED landing lights.
Simplified Pilot Workload

The combination of the ECU and the MT Constant Speed Propeller automates engine adjustments that previously required constant manual oversight. By reducing required engine adjustments in-flight, the system allows the pilot to focus on navigation and situational awareness during critical flight phases.

6. Professional Justification and Conclusion

The engineering data and regulatory framework support a definitive business case for the upgrade. This project effectively resets the aircraft's lifecycle, transforming it into a modern, competitive asset.

In conclusion, the Rotax 912iSc3 modernization transforms the Grob G 109B from a maintenance-heavy legacy airframe into a high-performance, technologically advanced motor glider. By investing in this upgrade, operators secure a future-proof asset suitable for another 40 years of safe and efficient operation.
